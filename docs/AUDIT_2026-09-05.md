# Braillix full audit — 2026-09-05

Three independent audits examined the CAD, the simulator, and the electronics.
Each auditor worked without knowledge of the others. Each auditor received an
instruction to find defects in recent work, not to confirm it.

This document records the results. The detailed reports are in
`.ai-sync/artifacts/audit_cad_2026-09-05.md`, `audit_sim_2026-09-05.md`, and
`audit_elec_2026-09-05.md`.

## Decision

**Do not print the mechanism. Do not assemble a cell.**

The audit found six defects that stop the mechanism from working. Three are in
the CAD. Three are in the firmware. Four of the six were introduced or preserved
during the last week of changes.

The fit coupons remain safe to print. No coupon depends on any of the six
defects.

## Summary of severity

| Area | Blockers | Major | Verified correct |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| CAD | 3 | 6 | winding fix, blind bore, teardrop, dwell offset |
| Simulator | 1 | 6 | braille logic, Gray order, all 64 states |
| Electronics | 4 | 8 | strapping pins clean |

## 1. CAD

### BLOCKER C1 — The comb blocks the linkages

The comb pocket for each foot stops 0.05 mm below the top of the comb. A thin
roof covers all six pockets. You cannot put the linkages into the comb.

```
pocket cut runs z  0.75 .. 3.25
comb top is              3.30
material over pocket    +0.05 mm
```

The auditor measured the intersection of the two solids as 0.460 mm³ across six
separate bodies at z 3.250 to 3.300.

**This defect came from the change of `pin_lift` from 0.8 mm to 0.5 mm.** The
comb computes `clear_z = pin_lift + 0.35`. At 0.8 mm the cut ended 0.25 mm above
the comb top, so the pockets were open. At 0.5 mm the cut ends 0.05 mm below it.

Fix: derive the pocket height from the comb, not from the lift. The cut must
always pass through the top face.

### BLOCKER C2 — The cam has no clearance under it

The underside of the cam disc and the floor of its pocket are both at z 31.5.
The clearance is exactly zero across the full 48.4 mm disc.

```
disc underside     31.5
cam pocket floor   31.5
clearance          0.00 mm
```

`base_plate_z` contains no clearance term. Any tolerance in the print, the
motor, or the shaft makes the disc rub on the plate. The cam must turn freely.

This condition existed before the 2.5 mm stack raise. The raise preserved it.

Fix: add a clearance term of 0.3 mm to 0.5 mm to `base_plate_z`, or lower the
pocket floor by the same amount.

### BLOCKER C3 — The Hall sensor pocket cuts through the plate

`hall_pocket_height` returns 4.6 mm. The material beneath the cam pocket is only
2.0 mm thick. The pocket therefore removes all of it. The 0.4 mm roof that the
design describes does not exist. The pocket is a window.

The guard assert compares 4.6 against 1.6. These are the wrong two quantities.
The assert passes more easily as the defect becomes worse.

Fix: compute the pocket depth from the material that remains under the cam
pocket. Correct the assert to compare the pocket depth against that material.

### MAJOR C4 — The ramp exceeds 30 degrees on three tracks

R-07 sized each ramp with a formula for a straight ramp. The cam profile uses a
cosine S-curve. The peak slope of an S-curve is π/2 times the mean slope.

| Track | Design target | Measured from the profile |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 29.5° | **41.6°** |
| 1 | 25.4° | **36.7°** |
| 2 | 22.3° | **32.8°** |

The three inner tracks exceed the 30 degree limit. R-07 improved the ramps by a
large amount, but it did not reach its own target.

Fix: divide the target angle by π/2 in the sizing formula, or change the profile
to a constant-velocity ramp with small blend radii.

### MAJOR C5 — The ramp has about 2.4 facets

`subdivisions_per_slice = 4` was chosen when a ramp was 20 percent of a slice. A
ramp is now 61 to 75 percent of a slice. The exported mesh describes the S-curve
with approximately 2.4 facets. The printed surface is not the designed surface.

Fix: raise `subdivisions_per_slice` to 12 or more. Manifold makes the cost small.

### MAJOR C6 — Two release parts contradict the design

1. `cam_linkage_test_fixture.scad` hard-codes `cam_d = 44.4` and `well = 45.0`.
   The disc is 48.4 mm. The file does not include `mech_layout.scad`. This part
   is in the validated coupon set and its G-code is published.
2. `mid_plate` is documented as superseded but remains in the released G-code.
   The comb is in no print set and no validator manifest.

### MAJOR C7 — The torque budget does not close

The motor supplies about 30 mN·m. By the project's own spring model, six springs
of 0.20 mm wire still consume about 17 mN·m before any dot moves.

The coil count of the springs is recorded nowhere. Spring rate depends on coil
count. The rate therefore has a spread of about three times.

### MINOR C8 — Other findings

- The dock magnet assert applies the teardrop growth to Y. The apex points +Z.
  The Z extent is unchecked.
- The comb has no spigot. Two files state that it has one. `spigot_r` feeds a
  cylinder of height −0.05 mm, which produces nothing.
- About 18 numeric comments are stale, including three arm-span tables that
  disagree with each other and with the code.

### Verified correct

The auditor confirmed the polyhedron winding fix, the blind bore and its 0.5 mm
roof, the teardrop orientation, `DWELL_OFFSET = 32`, and that the committed STL
files match fresh renders.

## 2. Simulator

### BLOCKER S1 — The 3D models are older than the CAD

The GLB files were built on 2026-09-04 at 02:58. The STL files were rebuilt on
2026-09-05 at 13:40. Seven of nine parts have different triangle counts from the
mesh they claim to show.

The simulator therefore draws old geometry and labels it with new numbers. This
is the exact condition the simulator exists to prevent.

**This defect came from the STL rebuild.** The rebuild did not regenerate the
GLB files.

Fix: regenerate the GLB files after every STL export. Add the step to the export
script so it cannot be forgotten.

### MAJOR S2 — The environment map is not installed

`sim/3d/app.js:319` reads:

```js
scene.environment = pmrem.fromScene(env, 0.035)   // >0.04 clips: ... at 20.texture;
```

The text `.texture;` is inside the trailing comment. The assignment therefore
receives a `WebGLRenderTarget` and not a `Texture`. Every `envMapIntensity`
value in both files multiplies nothing. The metal surfaces reflect black.

Fix: move `.texture;` outside the comment.

### MAJOR S3 — The simulator shows parts that do not exist

- It draws a DC barrel jack. The CAD replaced it with a 6 × 4 mm cable slot.
- A callout states "Polarity verified: centre positive" for that jack.
- The ESP32 board sits 6.0 mm too low, below its own USB opening.
- Callouts state "68x68x58" for a pod that is 46.5 mm tall, magnets "at y ±14"
  when the CAD uses ±18.5, and a "13-wire loom" when 17 wires are drawn.

### MAJOR S4 — Braille conversion defects in the firmware helper

`firmware/braille_converter.py` omits the letter sign, so "1a" reads as "11". It
repeats the number sign inside one number, so "1,000" emits it twice.
Parentheses lack the dot-5 prefix cell. The simulator performs these correctly.

### MAJOR S5 — Most parameters are hand-typed

`extract_params.py` extracts 20 mechanism values. About 60 more are typed by
hand into `electronics.js` and `renders/*.py`. The motor block in
`export_glb.py` is already wrong: it uses 28.0, −8.0 and 35.0 against measured
values of 28.1, −7.5 and 34.7.

### Verified correct

All 64 cam states settle at exactly 0.000 mm or 0.500 mm on all six dots. Gray
order round-trips. The dot-to-bit map agrees across the CAD, the JSON, the
JavaScript, and the firmware. The two `braillix_params.json` files are both
generated and identical. The mobile layout is sound.

## 3. Electronics and firmware

### BLOCKER E1 — The step count is wrong for this motor

The 28BYJ-48 gearbox ratio is 63.68395 : 1. It is not 64 : 1.

```
half-steps per output revolution   64 x 63.68395 = 4075.77
firmware uses                                      4096
error per revolution                               20.2 steps
```

`braille_cell.ino:73` states `// 28BYJ-48 half-steps, 64:1 gearbox`. The comment
records the wrong ratio.

Against the dwell budget from R-07:

```
steps per state, assumed   64.000
steps per state, actual    63.684
drift per state             0.316
dwell budget               +/-4.2
```

A dot lands on a ramp after about 13 state changes. The device works for about a
dozen characters, then puts dots at partial height, and becomes worse until the
next power cycle.

Fix: use 4076 steps per revolution, or re-home at a fixed interval, or both.

### BLOCKER E2 — The Hall sensor runs below its minimum voltage

`BUILD_PACK.md` section 3.4 permits only a 3.3 V supply for the A3144. The
datasheet minimum is 4.5 V.

Fix: supply the A3144 from 5 V. Pull its open-collector output up to 3.3 V. The
open-collector output makes the pull-up rail set the high level, so the logic
input never sees 5 V.

### BLOCKER E3 — Three files define homing three different ways

The CAD requires the first edge of the field. `braille_cell.ino` uses the
minimum of `analogRead`. `breadboard_test.ino` uses the midpoint. The newer
sketch contains the regression.

The magnet spans 26.7 degrees, which is 4.7 states. The minimum of the field is
therefore meaningless. Nothing subtracts the half-window offset of about 2.4
states.

GPIO34 has no internal pull-up. A missing external resistor gives a floating pin
and a silent false home.

### BLOCKER E4 — The ground offset calculation counts one joint

`BUILD_PACK.md:534` computes the drop across a single dock joint. The offset
accumulates along the chain.

```
stated at 8 cells      100 mV
actual at 8 cells      450 mV
```

The I2C low-level margin runs out at about six simultaneous cells. Sequential
refresh reduces the offset to 0.2 V at eight joints and solves the problem, but
nothing in the firmware enforces sequential refresh.

### MAJOR E5 — The pin conflict is still live

`braille_cell.ino:66-67` uses GPIO21 and GPIO22 for IN3 and IN4. These are the
default I2C pins. `BUILD_PACK.md:240-241` and `ELECTRONICS_PLAN.md:104-116`
specify GPIO23 and GPIO27. The firmware contradicts both documents.

The strapping pins 0, 2, 5, 12 and 15 are clean.

### MAJOR E6 — The expander part number is impossible in every document

Every document specifies a DIP-28 MCP23017. `electronics_spec.scad:98` asserts
that the board length is 34 mm or less. A DIP-28 body is 35.6 mm. The part
cannot fit.

The owned SOIC-28 part is the correct choice. Its adapter board, however,
consumes the 1.35 mm height margin at `electronics_spec.scad:96`.

### MAJOR E7 — Other findings

- `runToTarget()` blocks inside the BLE write callback for seconds and never
  yields. This drops links and can trigger the watchdog.
- `shortestTarget()` reverses direction on about half of all moves. Gearbox
  backlash of about 1 degree, or 11.4 steps, is never removed. A fixed-direction
  final approach removes it.
- The instruction to connect ULN2003 pin 9 to +5 V appears in no document. The
  claim that no flyback diodes are needed depends on that connection.
- A WiFi password is hardcoded at `braille_cell.ino:54`. `USE_WIFI` is false, so
  the risk is low. The unauthenticated motor API with `Allow-Origin: *` is the
  larger issue.

### Disputed

One auditor states that a 3.3 V MCP23017 cannot drive a ULN2003 input. The
ULN2003 input is a 2.7 kΩ resistor into a Darlington pair. At 3.3 V this gives
about 0.7 mA of base current, which is sufficient for 200 mA coils. The
combination is common with ESP32 boards.

A meter settles this. It is on the bench list.

## 4. Proposed features

The ESP32 constrains what is possible. These proposals respect that constraint.

### Recommended: read braille files from a microSD card

A microSD card reader uses SPI and four wires. It costs about ₹60.

**Use BRF files, not PDF.** BRF is the Braille Ready Format. Each byte is one
braille cell. The file needs no translation and no font. Libraries such as
Project Gutenberg and Bookshare publish BRF files at no cost.

PDF is not practical. A PDF reader must handle compression, fonts, and page
layout. The ESP32 does not have the memory for this work.

The classic ESP32 has no USB host controller. A USB flash drive therefore needs
an ESP32-S3 or a separate host chip. A microSD card gives the same result with
less hardware.

### Recommended: remember the reading position

Write the byte offset of the current cell to non-volatile storage. The device
then resumes at the same place after a power cycle. This costs about 10 lines of
code and no hardware.

### Recommended: practice mode

The device shows a random character. The user presses a button to hear a beep
pattern that confirms or rejects the answer. This needs no additional hardware.
It demonstrates the complete system in one minute.

### Buzzer cue vocabulary

The buzzer exists in the plan with no defined behaviour. This vocabulary uses
one pin and about 45 lines of non-blocking code.

| Event | Cue |
|---|---|
| Button accepted | One short beep. Lift your finger. |
| Motion complete | Two short beeps. The cell is safe to read. |
| Home successful | Three rising beeps. |
| Home failed | Two long beeps. Do not trust the character. |
| End of file | Four short beeps. |

Keep both tones between 2.2 kHz and 3.0 kHz. A piezo transducer inside a case
does not reproduce 400 Hz at a useful volume. Carry the meaning in the number of
beeps, not in the pitch.

### Possible: spoken letter names

An I2S amplifier and a small speaker can play recorded audio clips from the SD
card. Speech synthesis on the device is not practical. Recorded clips of the 26
letter names are practical and are useful for teaching.

Make speech optional. A device that speaks every character removes the reason to
read braille.

### Possible: battery power

A single 18650 cell and a charging module make the device portable. Portability
matters for a device that a student carries to class.

## 5. Recommended order of work

1. Correct the three CAD blockers. Each is a small parameter change.
2. Correct the step count in the firmware. This is one constant.
3. Rebuild the STL files and regenerate the GLB files together.
4. Print the fit coupons. They are safe now.
5. Measure one spring: free length, coil count, and force at working
   compression. The spring decision depends on this measurement.
6. Correct the ramp angle formula and raise `subdivisions_per_slice`.
7. Reconcile the documents against the CAD.

## 7. Print-release ladder

Carried forward from the 2026-09-04 audit, which this document replaces.

| Gate | Evidence required | Allowed output |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Current | Spring and dock architecture unresolved | No final parts |
| 1. Freeze | Tested spring path; selected controller/pogo/buzzer; fixed pin map | Documentation only |
| 2. Fit | Re-sliced coupons; actual motor/Hall/board/pogo dry-fits | PETG coupons only |
| 3. Mechanism | One-dot and six-dot travel, dwell, return, 50 cycles, no stall | Resin fixture set |
| 4. Dock | Two powered cells, polarity, retention, voltage drop, sequential refresh | One enclosure set |
| 5. Release | All evidence recorded | Full batch and final G-code |

The checked G-code profiles have acceptable settings, but all 11 files hash to older STLs. Re-slice the current coupon G-code after specifications are frozen; do not call the existing files current.


## 8. What the audit could not check

No auditor had access to hardware. The following need a bench:

- Running torque and backlash of the real motor
- Spring rate of the owned springs
- Whether a 3.3 V expander drives the ULN2003 correctly
- Magnetic holding force at the dock
- Whether the browser reports an error for the environment map defect

The Blender connection failed, so `sim/3d/motor.glb` was not examined.

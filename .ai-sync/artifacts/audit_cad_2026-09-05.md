## Summary

The R-07 pass and the winding fix are genuinely good work and I could confirm both:
the cam mesh is now identical under Manifold and CGAL (4190.042 mm3), the blind shaft
bore is real with a 0.5 mm roof, the teardrop magnet apex really does point at +Z,
and there is **no** interference between the six linkages, the top plate, the dot
insert or the base plate at either dot position.

However, the same pass left three defects that stop the mechanism from being built or
turned, and none of them is caught by `tools/validate_print_assets.py`, which currently
reports PASS:

1. The Hall-sensor recess is not a recess. It is a 4.5 x 3.5 mm **through-window** in
   the cam-pocket floor. Its guard assert compares two quantities that have nothing to
   do with the failure.
2. All six comb foot pockets are **capped by a 0.05 mm roof**. The linkages physically
   cannot be dropped into the comb.
3. The rotating cam disc's underside is **exactly coplanar** with the base-plate cam
   pocket floor. Nominal axial running clearance is 0.000 mm across the full 48.4 mm
   disc.

Beyond that, the two headline claims of the recent work do not hold up numerically.
R-07 is justified throughout by a 30-degree pressure-angle limit, but the profile is a
cosine S-curve and its **peak** slope on the inner track is 41.6 degrees. And the
0.20 mm spring-wire change reduces the torque problem roughly five-fold but does not
close it, because no coil count is specified anywhere, so the spring rate is undefined.

The comb — the one part whose own header says the mechanism does not work without it —
appears in no print set and in no validator manifest, while a part the repo documents
as superseded (`mid_plate`) is still in the released G-code set.

## BLOCKER

### B-1. The Hall sensor pocket is a through-window in the cam pocket floor

**What is wrong.** `cad/scad/hall_interface.scad:23` computes
`hall_pocket_height(0, 5) = plate_thickness + 0 - hall_floor_t = 4.6`, and
`hall_sensor_pocket()` cuts a 4.5 x 3.5 mm box from `z = -0.1` up to `z = 4.5`.
At the sensor location `(0, 17.35)` the base plate is only **2 mm** thick, because
`cam_features()` in `cad/scad/base_plate.scad:216` has already removed everything from
`z = base_thickness - cam_pocket_depth = 2` upward over a 50 mm circle. The pocket
therefore consumes the entire remaining 2 mm floor.

**Evidence (VERIFIED).** Intersecting the rendered `base_plate()` with the exact pocket
footprint returns a degenerate zero-volume shell:

```
pocket x[-2.25,2.25] y[15.6,19.1] depth=4.6
material available under cam pocket = 2mm
hall2.stl  tris=16  vol=-0.000  x[-2.250,2.250] y[15.600,19.100] z[0.000,2.000]
```

A second probe over `x[-2,2] y[16,18.7] z[-1,9]` returned "Current top level object is
empty" — there is no material at all in that column.

**Why it matters.** `base_plate.scad:151-160` states the design intent explicitly:
"the pocket is cut into the plate's UNDERSIDE ... leaving a thin membrane between
sensor and cam", with `hall_floor_t = 0.4`. That membrane does not exist. The TO-92
sensor sits in an open window with nothing above it, directly under a disc whose
underside is coplanar with that face (see B-3), so the sensor can lift into the disc
and jam it. This is the identical failure mode the team already fixed on the cam side
(the 2 mm magnet pocket that cratered tracks 2-4), reintroduced on the plate side.

**The assert is checking the wrong thing.** `base_plate.scad:186`:

```
assert(hall_pocket_h >= hall_body_t,
       "Hall sensor is thicker than the base plate can recess it. ...");
```

This asks "is 4.6 >= 1.6", which is trivially true and gets *more* true as the bug gets
worse — a deeper pocket makes it pass harder. The comment two lines above ("M11b equals
the available 1.6mm recess exactly") shows someone knew the available depth is 1.6 mm,
but that 1.6 never enters the assert.

**Fix.** Make the available depth explicit and derive the pocket from it:

```
hall_available = base_thickness - cam_pocket_depth;              // 2.0
function hall_pocket_height(rr, t) = rr == 4 ? (existing) : hall_available - hall_floor_t;  // 1.6
assert(hall_available - hall_floor_t >= hall_body_t,
       "no room to recess the Hall body under the cam pocket");
```

1.6 mm of recess for a 1.6 mm body leaves zero clearance, so the honest options are to
thin `hall_floor_t` to 0.3 and accept 1.7, thicken the plate locally with a downstand
boss, or move the sensor outboard of the cam pocket and re-site the magnet. Choose one
deliberately; do not let the assert decide.

### B-2. Every comb foot pocket is capped by a 0.05 mm roof — the linkages cannot be fitted

**What is wrong.** In `cad/scad/linkage_comb.scad`:

- `comb_top = arm_y - 0.2 = 3.3`, `rest_z = 1.0`, so `comb_h = 2.3` and the solid spans
  `z = 1.0 .. 3.3`.
- `foot_pocket()` cuts `translate([..., clear_z - 0.1]) cube([pocket_l, pocket_w, comb_h + 0.2])`
  with `clear_z = pin_lift + 0.35 = 0.85`, so the cut spans `z = 0.75 .. 3.25`.

3.25 is 0.05 mm short of the 3.3 comb top. Every other cut in the file (central bore,
peg holes, standoff scallops) starts at `rest_z - 0.1 = 0.9` and so runs to 3.4 and
clears; only the foot pockets start from `clear_z`, which is *below* `rest_z`.

**Evidence (VERIFIED).** Intersecting the rendered comb with six probe columns at the
foot positions:

```
roof.stl  tris=72  Genus=-5 (6 separate bodies)  vol=0.495  z[3.250, 3.300]
```

And the direct assembly interference check, six linkages against the comb:

```
lk_comb_0  vol=0.460  z[3.250,3.300]     (dot down)
lk_comb_5  vol=0.420  z[3.250,3.300]     (dot up)
```

The linkage lower riser is embedded in comb material over exactly the 0.05 mm band.

**Why it matters.** The comb's own header says "the arm sits at arm_y and the comb tops
out below it, so a linkage lowers straight in". It does not. On resin a 0.05 mm film
will either print as a closed membrane that blocks assembly, or vanish and leave a
ragged, out-of-tolerance pocket mouth. Either way the pocket is not the four-walled
closed pocket the design depends on.

**Fix.** Cut the pockets through, from below the comb to above it:

```
module foot_pocket(d) {
    rotate([0, 0, dot_phase_of(d)])
        translate([track_r(dot_track_of(d)) - pocket_l/2, -pocket_w/2, rest_z - 0.5])
            cube([pocket_l, pocket_w, comb_h + 1.0]);
}
```

and add `assert(clear_z - 0.1 + comb_h + 0.2 > comb_top + 0.1, "foot pockets are blind");`

### B-3. The rotating cam disc has zero axial clearance to the base plate

**What is wrong.** The stack closes so tightly that the disc underside lands exactly on
the cam-pocket floor:

| datum | z (world) | source |
|---|---|---|
| box floor top | 4.0 | `outer_box.scad` |
| motor mounting face | 23.0 | + `motor_can_h = 19.0` |
| shaft boss top = hub bottom | 25.0 | + `motor_boss_h = 2.0` |
| disc underside | 31.5 | + `hub_h = 6.5` |
| base plate underside | 29.5 | `base_plate_z` |
| **cam pocket floor** | **31.5** | + `base_thickness - cam_pocket_depth = 2` |
| cam flat | 33.5 | `cam_flat_z` |

**Evidence (VERIFIED).** Intersecting the cam placed on its own datum with the base
plate placed on its own datum:

```
e_cam_base.stl  tris=416  vol=-0.000  x[-24.152,24.200] y[-24.200,24.200] z[-2.000,-2.000]
```

A zero-volume coplanar contact across the whole disc diameter. `base_plate_z = 29.5` is
literally derived as `4 + 19 + 2 + 6.5 - 3 + 1` with no clearance term.

**Why it matters.** Three separate consequences.

1. Nominally the disc's whole 48.4 mm underside sits on the plate. Any negative
   tolerance in the motor can height (`motor_can_h = 19.0`, a single measured value
   with no stated tolerance) or in resin shrink pushes the disc onto the plate and the
   hub off the boss. Half the tolerance band grinds. `mech_layout.scad` already claims
   17 N of spring load pressing the disc down — dragging that over a Ø48 annulus of
   PETG is far more friction than the tracks.
2. The mechanism's whole axial datum is a chain of five measured/assumed numbers with
   no adjustment feature and no clearance anywhere.
3. Coincident faces of this size are the exact meshing hazard that
   `dock_interface.scad:100-108` documents at length for the pogo pocket. Here it is
   again, on a much bigger area.

**Fix.** Introduce an explicit running gap. Deepen the cam pocket, or raise the plate:

```
cam_axial_gap = 0.5;                       // running clearance under the disc
cam_pocket_depth = 3 + cam_axial_gap;      // pocket floor drops 0.5 below the disc
assert(cam_pocket_depth < base_thickness - 1.2, "cam pocket leaves too little plate");
```

Note this interacts with B-1: deepening the pocket makes the Hall floor thinner still,
which is why the two must be re-derived in one pass.

### M-2. The 30-degree pressure-angle limit that R-07 is built on is exceeded on half the tracks

R-07's rationale in `braille_cam.scad:88-118` sizes `foot_flat_arc` as
`2 * (foot_roll_r * tan(15) + dwell_tol)` — that is, it assumes each ramp is a straight
30-degree face. But `get_height_at_angle()` applies `s_curve(t) = (1 - cos(180t))/2`,
whose maximum derivative is pi/2 times its average. The peak pressure angle is therefore
about 57 percent higher than the number the sizing formula targets.

**Evidence (VERIFIED — measured from the real profile function, 20000 samples/rev):**

```
track 0  CONTINUOUS max slope = 41.585 deg     (r=14.80, ramp arc 0.885 mm)
track 5  CONTINUOUS max slope = 24.548 deg     (r=23.30, ramp arc 1.720 mm)
```

Computed for all six from the same formulas:

| track | r | ramp arc | mean angle | **peak angle** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 14.80 | 0.885 | 29.5 | **41.6** |
| 1 | 16.50 | 1.052 | 25.4 | **36.7** |
| 2 | 18.20 | 1.219 | 22.3 | **32.8** |
| 3 | 19.90 | 1.386 | 19.8 | 29.5 |
| 4 | 21.60 | 1.553 | 17.9 | 26.8 |
| 5 | 23.30 | 1.720 | 16.2 | 24.5 |

Tracks 0, 1 and 2 exceed the file's own stated limit. Track 0 — driven by dot 5, which
`cam_linkage_test_fixture.scad` correctly identifies as the worst case — is at 41.6
degrees, which is close to the self-locking region for a dry resin-on-resin sliding
pair (mu ~0.3 gives a friction angle near 17 degrees; a 41.6-degree face with a
0.5 mm-radius roller that in practice slides rather than rolls is marginal).

Secondary consequence: `foot_flat_arc` also assumes 30 degrees when computing the flat
the roller consumes (`foot_roll_r * tan(15) = 0.134`). At 41.6 degrees the true value is
`0.5 * tan(20.8) = 0.190`, so the dwell is 0.11 mm narrower than budgeted as well.

**Fix.** Either solve for the fraction that makes the *peak* meet the limit —
`ramp_arc >= pin_lift * (pi/2) / tan(30) = 1.360 mm` — which tracks 0, 1 and 2 cannot
supply at `pin_lift = 0.5` and 64 states; or switch the ramp to a constant-velocity
(linear) segment with small blends, which recovers the mean-equals-peak assumption the
formula already makes. Add the check so it cannot drift:

```
function peak_ramp_angle(t) = atan(pin_lift * PI/2 / (ramp_fraction_of(t)*slice_arc(t)));
for (t=[0:dots-1]) assert(peak_ramp_angle(t) <= 30, str("track ",t," peak pressure angle ", peak_ramp_angle(t)));
```

### M-3. The ramp is meshed with about 2.4 facets, so the S-curve is not what gets printed

`subdivisions_per_slice = 4`, and the ramp now occupies 61-75 percent of a slice.

**Evidence (VERIFIED):**

```
track 0  AS-MESHED max facet slope = 33.47 deg   (4 samples/slice, ramp spans 2.436 of them)
track 5  AS-MESHED max facet slope = 23.25 deg   (4 samples/slice, ramp spans 3.007 of them)
```

The whole point of R-07 was that the follower must climb a gentle, smooth ramp. At 2.4
facets the disc has a faceted ramp with hard slope discontinuities, and the chord also
cuts into the dwell flat at each end. This was a reasonable setting when
`angular_ramp_fraction = 0.2` (0.8 facets of ramp, 3.2 of flat — coarse but the flat
dominated); it is no longer.

**Fix.** Raise `subdivisions_per_slice` to 16 or more. Cost check: the current cam is
9408 triangles and renders in 0.325 s under Manifold; 16 gives roughly 37600 triangles,
which is nothing.

### M-4. The torque budget does not close, and the spring rate is undefined

`mech_layout.scad:230-247` argues that 0.30 mm wire loads the cam with ~17 N and ~87
mN*m of friction against a 30 mN*m motor, and that 0.20 mm wire "cuts it ~5x". The
factor is right (`(0.3/0.2)^4 = 5.06`), but the conclusion is never restated and the
resulting number is not comfortable.

Taking the file's own model at face value: 17 / 5.06 = 3.4 N of spring load,
`0.27 * 3.4 N * 19 mm ≈ 17 mN*m`. That is **57 percent of the motor's rated 30 mN*m
consumed by spring friction alone**, before the ramp climb, before the disc rubbing on
the plate (B-3), and before any allowance for the fact that a 28BYJ-48's usable running
torque at speed is well below its quoted holding figure. Adding the ramp term at the
inner track (normal load ~1.5 N against a 41.6-degree face gives ~1.3 N tangential at
r = 14.8, about 19 mN*m) puts the total above the motor's rating.

**The rate is not actually specified.** `spring_od`, `spring_wire` and `spring_free_l`
are given; **coil count is not**, and rate goes as `1/n`. Using
`k = G d^4 / (8 D^3 n)` with G = 70 GPa, d = 0.20, D = 1.80:

| active coils | k (N/mm) | force at 1.0 mm (dot down) | 6-spring load |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.80 | 0.80 N | 4.8 N |
| 5 | 0.48 | 0.48 N | 2.9 N |
| 9 | 0.27 | 0.27 N | 1.6 N |

`spring_free_l`'s comment says "~5 coils", which lands at the stiff end. There is a
3x spread hiding inside a parameter set that looks fully specified.

Worse, `top_plate.scad:169` demands "Spring constant: as soft as possible
(<= 0.1 N/mm)". That is **geometrically impossible** at this size: it needs about 24
active coils, whose solid height at 0.20 mm wire is 4.8 mm, longer than the 4.0 mm free
length. The stated requirement cannot be met by any spring that fits.

**Fix.** Specify the spring completely and assert on it:

```
spring_coils_active = 9;
spring_G            = 70000;                          // N/mm2, 302 stainless
spring_rate = spring_G * pow(spring_wire,4) / (8 * pow(spring_od - spring_wire,3) * spring_coils_active);
assert(spring_coils_active * spring_wire < plate_under_y + spring_recess_depth - (flange_top_y + pin_lift) - 0.2,
       "spring goes solid at full dot lift");
assert(6 * spring_rate * (spring_free_l - (plate_under_y + spring_recess_depth - flange_top_y)) * 0.3 * 19 < 20,
       "spring friction torque exceeds the 28BYJ-48 budget");
```

and correct the `<= 0.1 N/mm` line in `top_plate.scad` to whatever is achievable.

### M-5. The 0.30 mm spring conflict: a geometric fix exists, a torque fix does not

The brief asks for an honest assessment. Facts first, all VERIFIED:

- Nub diagonal `sqrt(nub_width^2 + link_thickness^2) = sqrt(1.0 + 1.0) = 1.41421 mm`.
- `spring_id = spring_od - 2*spring_wire`; at 0.20 mm wire this is **1.60**, at 0.30 mm
  it is **1.40**.
- `linkage.scad:78` asserts `diagonal <= spring_id - 0.05`. At 1.60 that is 1.414 <=
  1.55, passing with 0.136 mm to spare. At 1.40 it is 1.414 <= 1.35 and the render
  aborts. This assert is correct and is the only thing standing between the project and
  a jammed dot; do not weaken it.

**Geometry — a fix exists, and it is cheap.** The 1.414 mm diagonal is an artefact of
the nub being a *square prism*, because it inherits `link_thickness` as its extrusion
depth. The bore is round. Necking the nub to a round Ø1.30 post above the flange passes
a 1.40 bore with 0.05 mm per side, changes nothing about the arm, and costs one module:

```
// replace the square upper riser above flange_top_y with a round post
translate([0, flange_top_y, thickness/2]) rotate([-90,0,0])
    cylinder(d = 1.30, h = (total_h - dot_r) - flange_top_y);
```

The alternative — a 2.1 mm OD spring at 0.30 mm wire, giving `spring_id = 1.50` — also
clears, but 2.1 mm is not a stock size and the dot-insert bore would go to 2.3 mm,
leaving 0.30 mm walls at 2.6 mm pitch.

**Torque — no fix exists.** At 0.30 mm wire, D = 1.70, and the maximum coil count that
still stays clear of solid at full dot lift is about 5 active. That gives
`k = 70000 * 0.0081 / (8 * 4.913 * 5) = 2.88 N/mm`, hence about 2.9 N per spring at
1.0 mm precompression and **17.3 N over six** — which is exactly the 17 N the file
already identifies as fatal. Reducing precompression to get the force down is not
controllable: at k = 2.88 the useful window is 0.05-0.10 mm of deflection, far inside
the free-length tolerance of any real spring.

**Honest conclusion.** With 0.30 mm wire the parts can be made to *fit*, but the
mechanism will not *turn*. The owned springs are not usable. The project needs either
0.20 mm wire (with a specified coil count — see M-4) or a different return mechanism
entirely. This should be recorded as a purchase, not left as an open conflict.

I could not check the owned springs' actual free length or coil count — neither is
recorded anywhere in `cad/scad` or the docs, and without the coil count no rate can be
computed for them either.

### M-7. The comb has no spigot, and the cut that was meant to make room for one silently does nothing

`linkage_comb.scad`'s header says: "A spigot under the middle drops into that pocket and
centres the part." `base_plate.scad:229` repeats it: "the spigot on its underside, which
sits in the cam pocket, centres it."

There is no spigot. `spigot_r` is used in exactly one place, the underside relief cut:

```
translate([0, 0, rest_z - 0.1])
    cylinder(r = spigot_r, h = clear_z - rest_z + 0.1);
```

`clear_z - rest_z + 0.1 = 0.85 - 1.0 + 0.1 = -0.05`. **A negative-height cylinder.**
OpenSCAD produced no warning (VERIFIED — a clean render of the file emits nothing), so
this is a silent no-op.

**Evidence (VERIFIED).** The rendered comb bounding box is `z[1.000, 3.790]` with a flat
underside; nothing projects below `rest_z`. Centring is therefore done entirely by four
Ø2.2 peg holes over Ø2.0 pegs — 0.2 mm of slop on a 26.9 mm radius, about 0.4 degrees of
rotational play, against a part whose entire job is to resist rotation.

Related: `assert(clear_z > pin_lift, "comb underside would strike the cam bumps")` is
vacuous. The real underside is `rest_z = 1.0`, not `clear_z = 0.85`, so the assert
guards a surface that does not exist. (The actual clearance is fine — 0.5 mm, VERIFIED
by `cam_comb` intersection returning empty — but by accident, not by the check.)

**Fix.** Either add the spigot the two files promise, or delete the claim from both and
tighten the peg fit. If the spigot is added, guard it:
`assert(clear_z < rest_z, "no room for a spigot below the comb")` inverted correctly,
plus a real interference check against the cam bumps.

### M-8. Homing contradicts the CAD's own stated requirement, and the datum is untied

`mech_layout.scad:181-190` is emphatic: an 8 mm magnet at r = 17.35 subtends 26 degrees,
which is 4.7 states wide, so "Homing on the CENTRE of that window is useless. Home by
always rotating the SAME direction and latching the FIRST edge of the field."

`firmware/braille_cell/braille_cell.ino:191-203` does the opposite:

```
int best = 4095; long bestAt = 0;
while (stepper.distanceToGo() != 0) { stepper.run(); int v = analogRead(HALL_PIN);
  if (v < best) { best = v; bestAt = stepper.currentPosition(); } }
```

This latches the **field minimum**, i.e. the magnet centre. In practice a saturating
sensor makes `bestAt` land on the first step that hits the ADC floor, which is
edge-like but noise-dependent and not repeatable. The prior audit
(`docs/INDEPENDENT_PREPRINT_AUDIT_2026-09-04.md`, blocking finding 4) flagged this; it
is unchanged.

**Second, separate problem: nothing ties the homed datum to slice 0.** The magnet is at
disc angle 90 degrees (`homing_mag_angle`) and the Hall sensor is at `(0, 17.35)`, i.e.
world +Y. After homing, step 0 means "magnet under sensor". The cam pattern's slice 0 is
defined by `track_phase(t)`, which has nothing to do with 90 degrees. There is no
`home_offset_steps` constant in either the CAD or the firmware (VERIFIED by grep), and
no statement of which rotation direction is positive. Whether `stateToStep(0)` lands on
slice 0 or slice 63 depends on a motor wiring sign that is documented nowhere.

**Credit where due:** `DWELL_OFFSET = 32` is **correct** and I want to be explicit about
it, because it is easy to mistake for a bug. `get_height_at_angle()` puts the dwell at
the *middle* of a slice (at `angle_in_slice = 0` the function returns
`lerp(prev, curr, s_curve(0.5))`, the mid-ramp value), so the firmware must offset by
half a slice, and it does. The CAD file never documents this; only the firmware does.

**Fix.** Change `if (v < best)` to a first-crossing latch on `HALL_THRESHOLD` with a
single fixed rotation direction; add `home_offset_steps` to `mech_layout.scad`, derive
it from `homing_mag_angle` and the chosen positive direction, and export it into
`sim/3d/braillix_params.json` so the firmware and simulator read the same number.

`assert(dock_mag_y + dock_mag_dia/2*sqrt(2) < 68/2 - 2, "magnet teardrop runs off the edge of the wall");`

The `sqrt(2)` term is the teardrop's apex extension — but the apex points at **+Z**, not
+Y. **VERIFIED** by bounding box, which is exactly what the file's own comment demands:

```
td.stl  x[0.000,1.210]  y[-4.200,4.200]  z[-4.200,5.940]
```

So in Y the pocket extends only 4.2 mm (the assert is over-conservative and passes
anyway), and the Z extension of 5.94 mm — the one that could actually run into the
chevron groove, the lid rebate or the cavity floor — is **not checked at all**. As it
happens the current values are safe (`dock_mag_z + 5.94 = 19.44`, floor at 4.0, wall top
at 42.5), but the guard would not catch it if `dock_center_z` moved.

Fix: split into a Y check using `dock_mag_dia/2` and a Z check using
`dock_mag_dia/2*sqrt(2)` against the real wall extents.

### N-2. Comb orientation dot breaks the "stops below the arm" invariant
`orientation_dot()` sits at `comb_top - 0.01` and is `mark_h = 0.5` tall, reaching
z = 3.79 (**VERIFIED** by bounding box) against an arm underside at `arm_y = 3.5`. It
happens to be safe because it is at radius 31.1 mm where no arm reaches, but the part
now violates the rule `comb_top = arm_y - 0.2` exists to enforce, and there is no assert
covering the mark. Sink it into the top face instead, or assert its radius.

### N-3. The spring seat ledge is 0.25 mm wide and the spring can fall into the dot hole
`dot_insert.scad` cuts a Ø2.2 spring bore and a Ø1.7 dot hole coaxially, so the annular
seat the spring bears on is only `(2.2 - 1.7)/2 = 0.25 mm` wide. The spring is Ø2.0 in a
Ø2.2 bore, so it can sit 0.2 mm off centre — at which point on one side the coil's inner
edge (r = 0.8 - 0.1 = 0.7) is inside the dot hole radius (0.85) and the spring end can
drop into the hole and foul the dome. Consider a Ø2.05 bore, or a 0.4 mm-wide seat by
counterboring the dot hole only in the flange.

### N-4. The dot presents a 1.41 mm base, marginally under the braille minimum
`dot_dome_dia = 1.5` is repeatedly described as "braille standard (1.44-1.6mm)". But the
dome is a full sphere and only a `pin_lift = 0.5` cap emerges above the reading surface.
The cap's diameter at the surface is `2*sqrt(0.75^2 - 0.25^2) = 1.414 mm` — just below
the 1.44 mm minimum the file cites. Raising `dot_dome_dia` to 1.6 gives a 1.52 mm base
and stays clear of the 1.7 mm hole.

### N-5. Dwell tolerance is probably below the 28BYJ-48's gearbox backlash
`dwell_tol = 0.15` mm of flat each side of a stop. At the inner track that is
`0.15/14.8 rad = 0.581 degrees` of output rotation; at the outer track 0.369 degrees.
A 28BYJ-48's half-step is 0.088 degrees, so step resolution is fine — but the geared
output backlash of these motors is commonly of the order of a degree, which would put
the foot onto the ramp on a direction reversal. `shortestTarget()` in the firmware takes
the shortest path, so reversals happen routinely. Either raise `dwell_tol`, or make the
firmware always approach a state from one direction. INFERRED — I have no backlash
measurement for the owned motor and none is recorded in the repo.

### N-6. `track_phase()` falls through silently
`mech_layout.scad:150-157` tests five equalities and returns `dot_phase[5]` for anything
else. It is correct for the current assignment (**VERIFIED**: 0/60/120/180/240/300 for
tracks 0-5), but a typo in `dot_track` would silently give track 5's phase to an
unrelated track and carve a whole track at the wrong angle with no error.
Add `assert(len(search(t, dot_track)) > 0, ...)` or build the inverse map explicitly.

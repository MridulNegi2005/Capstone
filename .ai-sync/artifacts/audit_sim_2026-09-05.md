# Independent audit — Braillix simulator and supporting tooling

Date: 2026-09-05
Scope: `sim/` (all), `sim/extract_params.py`, `firmware/`, `renders/*.py`,
`tools/validate_print_assets.py`, cross-checked against `cad/scad/*.scad` at HEAD `142844e`.
Method: static reading plus executed checks (Python, Node, direct GLB/STL binary parsing).
No project file was modified.

---

## Summary

The **kinematic core of the simulator is correct**. I executed the cam maths and every
one of the 64 states settles at exactly 0.000 or 0.500 mm on all six dots, the Gray
mapping round-trips, and `braillix_params.json` regenerates byte-identical from the
current CAD. Both copies of the params file agree. The dot-to-bit table is consistent
across four independent implementations (CAD, extractor, `app.js`, `braille_cell.ino`).

The problems are elsewhere, and they are all the same shape: **numbers and geometry that
did not come through `extract_params.py` have drifted.** Specifically:

1. `sim/3d/braillix.glb` and `sim/3d/pod.glb` are **two STL generations stale**. Seven of
   nine parts have a different triangle count than the STL they claim to be built from.
   The simulator is drawing pre-`ae738ff` geometry against post-`142844e` parameters —
   precisely the desync the repo's own comments say the tool exists to prevent.
2. The simulator draws a **DC barrel jack that no longer exists** in the CAD (replaced by
   a 6 × 4 mm cable slot), and a hotspot asserts "Polarity verified: centre positive".
3. The **ESP32 DevKit is placed 6.0 mm too low** inside the pod, below its own USB
   opening, because `boardUnderZ` was hand-copied from a v7.5 formula that the CAD has
   since replaced.
4. `scene.environment` is assigned a `WebGLRenderTarget` instead of its `.texture` — the
   intended `.texture` is swallowed inside a trailing comment. The whole
   "metals need something to reflect" design intent is silently inoperative.
5. Callout text asserts a 68 × 68 × **58** pod (it is 46.5), magnets at **y ±14**
   (they are ±18.5, and the file's own code says so), and a **13-wire** loom (17 are drawn).
6. `extract_params.py` extracts 20 mechanism numbers. Everything else the simulator
   needs — pod, box, dock, motor, driver, hall, pogo, shell heights — is hand-typed into
   `electronics.js` and `renders/*.py`, which is where all of the above went wrong.
7. `firmware/braille_converter.py` has real Grade-1 defects that the simulator's own
   `currentCells()` gets right, so the two disagree on the same input.

VERIFIED items were checked by running code or parsing files. INFERRED items are reasoned
from source only. **I have no browser automation, so nothing about actual rendered output,
frame rate, or runtime exceptions was observed.** Every claim about what the page *looks
like* is inferred from geometry and Three.js semantics.

# BLOCKER

## B-1. `braillix.glb` and `pod.glb` do not match the STLs they are built from

**VERIFIED** — by parsing the GLB and the STL binaries directly and comparing triangle counts.

| part | current `cad/stl/*.stl` | mesh inside the GLB | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `braille_cam` | 9 408 | 7 980 | **differs** |
| `outer_box` | 5 108 | 5 496 | **differs** |
| `esp32_pod_shell` | 2 634 | 2 608 | **differs** |
| `top_plate` | 2 750 | 2 744 | **differs** |
| `dot_insert` | 2 948 | 2 956 | **differs** |
| `linkage_asm_1` | 3 136 | 3 228 | **differs** |
| `esp32_pod_lid` | 1 178 | 1 176 | **differs** |
| `base_plate` | 4 608 | 4 608 | same |
| `linkage_comb` | 2 408 | 2 408 | same |

The counts differ in *both* directions (the GLB has 92 more triangles on `linkage_1` and
1 428 fewer on `cam`), so this cannot be explained by the glTF exporter dropping
degenerate faces. They are different meshes.

Git confirms the cause:

- `sim/3d/braillix.glb`, `sim/3d/pod.glb` — last rebuilt in `d3e4ce3`, **2026-09-04 03:01**
- `cad/stl/*.stl` — rebuilt in `a4bce47` (09-04 14:53), `ae738ff` (09-05 13:42) and
  `142844e` (09-05 13:43)

So the GLBs predate two full mesh regenerations, including `ae738ff`
("the track polyhedron was inside-out") which rewrote `braille_cam.stl`, and `142844e`
which rewrote `outer_box.stl` and `esp32_pod_shell.stl` after the ear-pocket/window
coplanarity fix.

**Why it matters.** `sim/3d/app.js:1-11` states the tool's entire warrant: *"if the printed
cam could not produce a motion, this app cannot show it."* That warrant is void while the
geometry on screen is not the geometry in `cad/stl/`. `sim/serve.py`'s docstring and
`loadGlb()`'s `cache: 'no-cache'` exist specifically to stop stale GLBs, and both were
defeated by simply not re-running the exporter. The parameters *were* regenerated
(verified below), so the page is currently old geometry driven by new numbers — the exact
failure mode both files warn about.

**Fix.**
1. Re-run `renders/export_glb.py` and `renders/export_pod_glb.py` against the current STLs.
2. Add a guard so this cannot recur silently. Cheapest version: have `export_glb.py` write
   a `sim/3d/glb_provenance.json` holding the SHA-256 of every source STL, and add a check
   to `tools/validate_print_assets.py` that re-hashes `cad/stl/*.stl` and fails if any
   hash used by a GLB is stale. That validator already hashes STLs for the G-code check
   (`tools/validate_print_assets.py:274-314`), so the machinery exists.
3. Consider having `app.js` fetch that provenance file and refuse to boot (or show a
   banner) when it does not match — the loader already has an error path (`main()`'s `err()`).

# MAJOR

## M-1. The simulator draws a DC barrel jack that the CAD deleted

**VERIFIED**

`sim/3d/electronics.js:24` declares `jack: { dia: 11.5, x: -20, y: 18 }`, `:135-144`
builds a full `barrelJack()` (flange, body, barrel, centre pin), `:683-686` places it on
the lid, and `:790-792` labels it:

> `['dc_jack', 'DC BARREL JACK 5.5/2.1', 'Power in, on the lid. Sits away from the board
> so the barrel hangs over open floor. Polarity verified: centre positive.']`

The CAD has no barrel jack. `cad/scad/esp32_pod_params.scad:66-74`:

> `// --- OWNED INLINE POWER PIGTAIL (through the LID) --- The photographed connector is a
> female inline lead, not a threaded panel jack. Keep its body outside the pod and pass
> only the red/black cable pair through this rounded slot.`
> `power_cable_slot_w = 6.0; power_cable_slot_h = 4.0; power_cable_x = -20; power_cable_y = 24;`

and `cad/scad/esp32_pod_lid.scad:33`: *"The old 11mm-deep guessed panel-jack cradle was
removed."* The project's own `sim/3d/MODELS.md` states the argument against sourced CAD
partly as *"Our jack is an inline pigtail, not the PCB-mount horizontal jack every library
ships"* — so the pipeline document knows, and the model does not.

The sim also has it at `y = 18`; the CAD slot is at `y = 24`.

**Why it matters.** This is the most direct form of "the simulator misleads": a 3D part
that does not exist, on the lid, with a clickable callout asserting a verified polarity for
a connector nobody owns. Anyone using the sim to plan the pod build wires the wrong part.

**Fix.** Delete `barrelJack()` and the `dc_jack` hotspot. Draw the 6 × 4 mm rounded slot
and the two-core cable passing through it, and give the callout the pigtail + tie-post
story (`esp32_pod_params.scad:66-74`, `tie_post_*`). Move `POD.jack` to
`POD.powerSlot = {w:6, h:4, x:-20, y:24}` so the name stops asserting a jack.

## M-2. The ESP32 DevKit sits 6.0 mm too low, below its own USB opening

**VERIFIED** — arithmetic, both sides.

`sim/3d/electronics.js:22`:
```
boardUnderZ: 10.5,   // :81 pod_floor+hdr_strip_h-channel
```
The CAD no longer computes it that way. `cad/scad/esp32_pod_params.scad:55,86`:
```
hdr_body_bottom_z = pod_floor + hdr_tail_space;      // 3 + 5 = 8
board_under_z     = hdr_body_bottom_z + hdr_strip_h; // 8 + 8.5 = 16.5
```
and `cad/scad/esp32_pod_assembly.scad:26` places the board at `board_under_z + 0.8`.
`hdr_channel_depth`, the term the sim's comment subtracts, **no longer exists anywhere in
`cad/scad/`** (grepped). The comment cites a v7.5 formula that was superseded.

Consequences, all computable:

- Sim board underside 10.5 vs CAD 16.5 → **6.0 mm low**.
- Sim header strips (`electronics.js:673-675`) span z 2.00 .. 10.50; CAD cradles them at
  z 8.00 .. 16.50. They are drawn sitting on the floor, not on the 5 mm tail-space ribs
  the shell actually prints (`esp32_pod_shell.scad:87-97`).
- The drawn USB-C socket (`esp32Devkit()`, `+1.6` above the board mid-plane) lands at
  z ≈ 11.3. The **real** shell's USB service opening is `usb_z = 14`, `usb_h = 12`,
  i.e. z 14 .. 26 — and `pod.glb` carries that real opening. **The modelled port is
  entirely below the modelled hole.**

`POD.usb = { w: 14, h: 9, z: 9.5 }` (`electronics.js:23`) is separately wrong — the CAD is
`usb_w = 14, usb_h = 12, usb_z = 14`. It is currently unreferenced (grepped: no `POD.usb`
uses), so it is wrong *and* dead, which is worse than either alone.

**Fix.** Set `boardUnderZ: 16.5` and `usb: {w:14, h:12, z:14}`, seat the header strips at
`pod_floor + hdr_tail_space` (8.0) rather than on the floor, and — the durable fix — emit
`pod_floor`, `hdr_tail_space`, `hdr_strip_h`, `board_under_z`, `usb_*` from
`extract_params.py` so this class of hand-copy stops (see M-6).

## M-3. `scene.environment` is set to a render target, not a texture

**VERIFIED** as a code defect; runtime effect **INFERRED**.

`sim/3d/app.js:319`, exactly as it appears in the file:
```js
scene.environment = pmrem.fromScene(env, 0.035)   // >0.04 clips: three caps the sample count at 20.texture;
```
The `.texture;` is inside the `//` comment. ASI terminates the statement at the end of the
line, so `scene.environment` receives the `WebGLRenderTarget` that `PMREMGenerator.fromScene()`
returns, not `renderTarget.texture`.

The author's intent is not in doubt — the trailing `.texture;` is the correct call, and it
was pushed into the comment by an edit.

**Runtime effect (INFERRED, three r185 — version literal confirmed as `"185"` in
`vendor/three.core.min.js`):** `WebGLCubeUVMaps.get()` passes through anything without
`.isTexture`, `envMap.mapping` is `undefined` so the cube-UV path is never selected, and
`WebGLTextures` then finds no `.image`. The expected result is a console warning and no
environment contribution — not an exception. That is consistent with `.ai-sync/handoff.md`
reporting the sim "loads and functions without application errors".

**Why it matters.** Three long comment blocks in this repo justify the environment map as
load-bearing:
- `app.js:311-314` — *"Without one, `metalness` near 1.0 reflects pure black … which made
  every braille dot look like a dark chrome bead smeared with red and blue."*
- `app.js:340-346` — *"Do not remove the env map."*
- `electronics.js:89-93` — *"The scene supplies a PMREM environment, so metals have
  something to reflect. Without bumping this the tiny parts read as flat grey chips."*

Every `envMapIntensity` tuned in both files (`ENV_INTENSITY = 1.0`, the 0.42→1.0
reconciliation, the wires' 0.7) is currently multiplying nothing. The linkages at
`metalness 0.85 / roughness 0.26` are the exact configuration those comments describe as
"dark smeared beads".

**Fix.** `scene.environment = pmrem.fromScene(env, 0.035).texture;` and move the comment to
its own line. Then re-check the material values, because they were tuned against a scene
that may never have had the env map working.

## M-4. Stale assertions in the part callouts, contradicted by code in the same file

**VERIFIED**

`sim/3d/electronics.js:789`
> `'A 68x68x58 shell, the same brick size as a cell…'`

The pod is 46.5 mm tall (`esp32_pod_params.scad:29`), and **line 18 of the same file says
`height: 46.5`**. `index.html:552` correctly says 46.5. The callout is the only place
still claiming 58.

`sim/3d/electronics.js:811`
> `['pod_magnets', 'DOCK MAGNETS 8x1mm', 'Two per face, flush with the dock wall at y +/-14…']`

The magnets are at **y = ±18.5** (`dock_interface.scad:233`, with an assert on line 234
that they clear the ear pocket), and **line 26 of the same file says `ys: [-18.5, 18.5]`**.
`dock_interface.scad:171-179` records exactly why ±14 was abandoned: the real 20.4 mm pogo
window overlaps a magnet at ±14 by 0.40 mm on the circle and 2.14 mm at the teardrop apex.
The callout therefore repeats a value the CAD explicitly rejected as an interference.

`sim/3d/electronics.js:812`
> `['cell_wiring', 'THE 13-WIRE LOOM', …]`

`cellHarness()` draws 5 (pogo) + 4 (IN1..IN4) + 5 (motor) + 3 (hall) = **17** tubes.

**Why it matters.** These are the clickable hotspots — the part of the tool a reviewer or
examiner reads instead of the CAD. A wrong magnet coordinate is directly actionable damage:
someone drilling or placing to y ±14 hits the pogo ear pocket.

**Fix.** Rewrite the three strings, and derive rather than retype:
`` `Two per face at y ±${POD.mag.ys[1]}` ``, `` `${L}×${W}×${H} shell` ``, and count the
wires from the group (`g.children.length`) rather than asserting a number.

## M-5. `firmware/braille_converter.py` disagrees with the simulator on real input

**VERIFIED** — by reading both implementations; the divergences are deterministic.

The simulator's `currentCells()` (`app.js:857-916`) implements three rules the Python
converter does not, so the two produce different cell sequences for the same string:

**(a) No letter sign — numbers followed by letters are ambiguous.**
`app.js:36` defines `SIGN.letter = [5,6]` and emits it whenever a letter follows a digit
(`app.js:906`). `braille_converter.py` has no letter sign at all, and `braille_mapping.py`
defines no `LETTER_INDICATOR`. So `"1a"` converts to `#`, `a`, `a` — which a reader parses
as the digits "11". This is a correctness bug in Grade 1, not a stylistic difference.

**(b) A comma or point inside a number restarts the number sign.**
`braille_converter.py:81-89`:
```python
if char.isdigit():      in_number_mode = True
elif char != ' ':       in_number_mode = False
```
Punctuation clears numeric mode, so `"1,000"` produces
`[3456] [1] [2] [3456] [245] [245] [245]` — a second number sign in the middle of one
number. Correct is `⠼⠁⠂⠚⠚⠚`, one number sign. `app.js:911-913` gets this right:
*"a . or , inside a number stays part of it (2.5, 1,000)"*.

**(c) Parentheses are wrong in both braille codes.**
`braille_mapping.py:104-105`:
```python
'(': (1, 2, 6),       # Opening parenthesis (same as closing)
')': (3, 4, 5),       # Closing parenthesis
```
In UEB, brackets are two cells: `⠐⠣` (dots 5 then 126) and `⠐⠜` (dots 5 then 345). The
table emits only the *second* cell of each, dropping the dot-5 prefix, so a reader sees a
bare `⠣` / `⠜`. In the older EBAE code both parentheses are dots 2356 — which is what the
comment *"(same as closing)"* is a leftover of, and the adjacent data now contradicts it.
`app.js`'s `PUNCT` has no parentheses at all, so the sim silently renders them as
"not in this chart".

**(d) No capital word sign.** `app.js:869-883` collapses an all-caps run into two capital
signs; the Python emits one capital sign per letter. Permitted in UEB either way, but it
means `"HELLO"` is 10 cells in Python and 7 in the sim — the two tools disagree on cell
count for the same word.

**Why it matters.** `hardware.js:8-12` states the design rule: *"The browser stays the
single source of truth for encoding… the simulator knows about capital and number
indicator cells and the firmware's a-z table does not."* That is a fine rule for the live
demo, but `braille_converter.py` + `main.py` is the piece presented as the software
deliverable, and it is the one with the defects. `main.py`'s `run_validation()` checks five
things, none of which would catch any of (a)–(d).

**Fix.**
1. Add `LETTER_INDICATOR = (5, 6)` to `braille_mapping.py` and emit it in
   `_convert_character` when a letter in `a`–`j` follows a digit.
2. Keep `in_number_mode` alive across `.` and `,` (`elif char not in ('.', ',')`).
3. Make parentheses two-cell (`(5,)` prefix) or drop them; fix the contradictory comment.
4. Add validation cases for `"1a"`, `"1,000"` and `"2.5"` to `main.py:run_validation()`.
5. Longer term: generate `LETTERS` for `app.js` from `braille_mapping.py` (it is a fourth
   copy — see M-6) rather than maintaining it by hand.

## M-6. `extract_params.py` covers the mechanism and nothing else; everything else is hand-copied

**VERIFIED**

The extractor is genuinely good at its stated job. It parses `col_spacing`, `row_spacing`,
`inner_radius`, `track_width`, `track_gap`, `pin_lift`, `cam_flat_z`, `plate_under_z`,
`plate_top_z`, `finger_pad_depth`, `dot_track`, `dot_phase`, `states`, `dots`,
`angular_ramp_fraction`, `disk_base_thickness`, `foot_roll_r`, `dwell_tol`,
`use_auto_ramp` — and it resolves `x = 45.0 + stack_repair_raise` through
`stack_options.scad`, which is a genuinely thoughtful piece of work.

**I re-ran it** (redirected to a scratch directory, no project file touched) and the output
is **byte-identical** to both `sim/braillix_params.json` and `sim/3d/braillix_params.json`.
Its self-checks pass: `DOT_TO_BIT` matches, three worked examples match, all 64 states are
a bijection.

But the simulator needs ~60 more CAD numbers, and none of them come through it. Every one
is typed by hand into a `.js` or a render script:

| `electronics.js:308-317` (`POGO`) | 23.0, 2.54, 5, 16.0, 20/4/2, 27/4/2, 2.1, 4.0, 4.1, 0.9/0.7/1.0 | `dock_interface.scad` |

`hall` section from `outer_box.scad`, `esp32_pod_params.scad`, `dock_interface.scad`,
`motor_spec.scad` and `electronics_spec.scad`, and have `electronics.js` read them from
`P` at boot the way `app.js` already reads `P.cam` and `P.stack`. Add `use_gray_order` to
the `cam` block immediately — it is a one-line change and closes the worst hole. Generate
the alphabet block for `app.js` from `braille_mapping.py` in the same pass.

## M-7. Homing sets zero at the magnet's leading edge; the CAD's datum is its centre, and nobody corrects the difference

**VERIFIED** as a code/geometry mismatch; the resulting mechanical error is **INFERRED**.

`cad/scad/mech_layout.scad:179-182`:

> *"CONSEQUENCE FOR FIRMWARE, and it matters: an 8mm magnet at r=17.35 subtends 26 degrees,
> which is 4.7 states wide. Homing on the CENTRE of that window is useless. Home by always
> rotating the SAME direction and latching the FIRST edge of the field."*

`firmware/braille_cell/braille_cell.ino:186-203` does not latch an edge. It sweeps one full
revolution and keeps the position of the **minimum analog reading**:

```cpp
int best = 4095; long bestAt = 0;
while (stepper.distanceToGo() != 0) {
  stepper.run();
  int v = analogRead(HALL_PIN);
  if (v < best) { best = v; bestAt = stepper.currentPosition(); }
}
```

Two problems compound:

1. `HALL_PIN` is read with `analogRead` but the part is an **A3144** — a digital
   open-collector switch (`electronics.js:809`, `base_plate.scad:119`). Its output is not
   proportional to field strength. Across the ~26° window it sits flat at 0, so `v < best`
   (strict) happens to keep the *first* sample of the plateau, i.e. accidentally an edge.
   Any noise, any bounce, or swapping in a genuinely ratiometric sensor moves `bestAt`
   anywhere inside a 4.7-state window. The behaviour is right by accident, not by design.
2. Even taking the leading edge, `homing_mag_angle = 90` (`mech_layout.scad:187`) describes
   the magnet **centre**. The leading edge is ~13.3° before it — about **2.4 states**.
   There is no `HOME_OFFSET_STEPS` anywhere in the firmware, the params file, or the
   simulator. Nothing subtracts the half-window.

**Why it matters.** A constant 2–3 state offset makes every character wrong on the real
cell in a way that looks like a *logic* bug — the demo would show consistent garbage, and
the obvious suspects (Gray order, `DOT_TO_BIT`, the dwell offset) are all correct. It would
cost a long debugging session. The simulator cannot catch it because **the simulator does
not model homing at all**: `gotoIndex()` sends a state index and assumes the cell's zero is
the cam's zero.

**Fix.**
1. Latch explicitly on the first LOW→ (or HIGH→LOW) transition and stop, rather than
   scanning for a minimum. Use `digitalRead` for the A3144, and state the sensor type in a
   comment next to `HALL_PIN`.
2. Add `home_edge_offset_steps` to `braillix_params.json`, computed by
   `extract_params.py` from `homing_mag_dia`, `homing_mag_r` and `steps_per_rev`:
   `round(steps_per_rev * degrees(asin(dia/2/r)) / 360)` ≈ 151 steps ≈ 2.4 states. Have
   the firmware add it after latching, so the constant is derived from the CAD and not
   typed twice.
3. Once that exists, give the simulator a visible "homing" state so the assumption is on
   screen rather than implicit.

# MINOR

## m-1. `removeUnit()` disposes geometry shared with every other cell

**VERIFIED** (code); harmlessness **INFERRED**.

`app.js:1049` (`addUnit`) is explicit that clones share geometry:
> *"The clone shares geometry and materials with the first, so a second cell costs
> transforms and draw calls, not memory."*

`app.js:1084-1085` (`removeUnit`) then does:
```js
u.root.traverse(o => { if (o.isMesh) o.geometry?.dispose?.(); });
```
which disposes the geometry still in use by unit 0 and by `unitTemplate`. Three.js will
re-upload the attributes on the next frame (the CPU-side arrays survive `dispose()`), so
the likely effect is a hitch, not a black screen — but it is a full re-upload of the 45 k
triangle mechanism every time the − button is pressed, and it directly contradicts the
comment two functions above.

**Fix.** Delete the dispose loop. Clones own nothing to free.

## m-2. `__braillix.setAngleTo()` and `snap()` do not drive the scene they claim to test

**VERIFIED**

`app.js:1454-1495` describes the debug hooks as *"the only way to test the real scene rather
than just the readout"*. They do not work:

- `setAngleTo(ch)` sets the **globals** `camDeg` and `targetDeg`. Nothing renders from
  those. `updateMechanism()` (`app.js:1294-1301`) loops `units` and reads `u.deg`, which is
  untouched. The very next `tick()` leaves `u.deg = u.target` unchanged and re-renders the
  old angle.
- `snap()` calls `updateMechanism(camDeg)` — `updateMechanism` takes **no parameters**
  (`app.js:1294`). The argument is discarded.
- `updateMechanismAt(deg)` (`app.js:1305-1312`), the one function that would honour a
  hand-set angle, is never called by anything.

So any verification run that used `setAngleTo()` + `snap()` or `linkZ()` to check dot
heights was reading the animation's current state, not the requested one. `lift()` and
`settledLifts()` are pure functions and *are* trustworthy.

`updateMechanismAt`'s comment also still says *"just 0.8mm higher"*; `pin_lift` has been
0.5 since R-07 (`mech_layout.scad:198`).

**Fix.** Make `setAngleTo` write `units.forEach(u => { u.deg = u.target = deg; u.move = null; })`,
drop the unused argument from the `snap()` call, and either wire up `updateMechanismAt` or
delete it.

## o-1. Mobile: the default path is fine; Electronics + 4 cells is the risk

**INFERRED** — no device or browser was available.

At 390 × 844 the layout looks sound: `#rail` becomes a 46 dvh bottom sheet with tabs
(`index.html:379-411`), the stage keeps 54 dvh, `dvh` avoids the iOS URL-bar trap, the
`@media (hover:none)` rule pins hotspot opacity to 0.85 (a real bug that was found and
fixed), 44 px hotspot targets via `::before`, `setPixelRatio(min(dpr,2))`, and
`ResizeObserver` on `#stage` rather than a `resize` guess. This is better mobile work than
most Three.js demos.

The risk is draw calls. `electronics.js` builds every component as an individual `Mesh`
with its own `BoxGeometry`/`CylinderGeometry` — no merging, no instancing — and
`vendor/utils/BufferGeometryUtils.js` is vendored but used only by `GLTFLoader`. Counting
the constructors: `uln2003()` alone creates ~118 meshes (77 of them perfboard pads),
`esp32Devkit()` ~90, `pogoConnector()` 26 (built three times per cell), plus 22 wire tubes.
That is roughly **350 meshes per cell**, and every one has `castShadow = true`, so each is
drawn twice per frame.

With Electronics off (the default — `cellElec.visible = false`, `app.js:459`) only the
~16 GLB meshes per unit draw, which is fine. With Electronics **on** and `MAX_UNITS = 4`
that is on the order of 1 400 meshes / 2 800 draw calls per frame on a phone.

**Fix if it proves to be a problem on device.** Merge the static per-cell electronics into
a handful of geometries with `BufferGeometryUtils.mergeGeometries()` at build time (they
never animate), or drop `MAX_UNITS` to 2 when `matchMedia('(max-width:840px)')` matches.
Measure first — I could not.

## o-2. `applyXray()` sets `needsUpdate = true` on every material, every frame of the tween

**VERIFIED** (code); cost **INFERRED**.

`app.js:815-830` runs each frame of the 0.34 s tween and ends with `m.needsUpdate = true`
unconditionally. That bumps `material.version` and forces the renderer to re-resolve the

**Fix.** Set `needsUpdate` only when `transparent` or `depthWrite` actually flips:


## o-3. The simulator omits the return springs, and overstates its own fidelity

**VERIFIED** (absence); **INFERRED** (effect on a viewer).

`mech_layout.scad:215-248` specifies six coaxial return springs (2.0 mm OD, 0.20 mm wire
since `f1db1d1`) as the thing that pulls each dot back down, and records that the wire
gauge is a *blocking* mechanical issue — six 0.3 mm springs loaded the cam with ~87 mN·m
against a 30 mN·m motor. Nothing in `sim/3d/` draws a spring or models one.

`app.js:1-7` claims: *"Nothing here is hand-animated: if the printed cam could not produce
a motion, this app cannot show it. That is the whole reason it is worth building."*

`linkageLift()` is a pure function of cam angle. It has no spring, no friction, no follower
inertia, no pressure-angle limit, no lost steps, no homing offset (M-7), and no backlash.
It shows the dot tracking the cam surface perfectly in both directions. So the app *can*
show motions the printed cam could not produce — every failure mode the CAD's own comments
worry about is invisible in it. That is fine for a teaching tool; the sentence is the
problem, because it is the tool's stated warrant and it is used to justify "catch design
errors before printing".

**Fix.** Draw the six springs (they are the answer to "what pulls the dot back?", which is
the obvious question a viewer asks), and soften the claim to what is actually true: the app
reproduces the cam's *surface profile* exactly, not its dynamics.
